## What Bonsai documents and what follows from it

Official sources below were read on 2026-10-08. Direct Bonsai requests returned HTTP 403. The `r.jina.ai` reader returned official-page HTML, including lists missing from its initial Markdown response. Claims here follow the complete HTML text. No live product account or customer workspace was used.

- ["Budget forecast based on task estimates"](https://help.hellobonsai.com/en/articles/10099494-budget-forecast-based-on-task-estimates), "When is a task included in Remaining Task Estimates?", requires a task not marked Done, due within the budget cycle, with an estimate greater than tracked time. Remaining estimate is task estimate minus tracked time. Consequently, an unfinished 10-hour task with 12 hours tracked is excluded by the stated condition. This is an inference from the documentation, not a measured runtime defect.
- The same article, "How is the budget forecast calculated?", says a time-budget forecast adds used budget and cumulative remaining estimates. Fee budgets add remaining estimates multiplied by estimated billable rate. The rate can come from member defaults, the project or a task's service. A billable rate measures a selling scenario, not internal cost or actual cash.
- The same article, "How are remaining task estimates assigned to the budget forecast?", assigns running-budget overdue work to today, other work to its due date, and completed-budget residual estimates to the budget's last day. That is a scheduling rule. It does not establish an independently validated completion-date prediction.
- ["Project budgets"](https://help.hellobonsai.com/en/articles/8916128-project-budgets), "What are project budgets", "How to set or update a project budget from project settings" and "What counts toward a project budget", distinguishes time and fee budgets and permits billable time, expenses or both. A budget's inclusion settings must be disclosed. Billable-time budget use does not establish all internal labor cost, including nonbillable rework.
- ["The Estimate builder"](https://help.hellobonsai.com/en/articles/10731093-the-estimate-builder), "Creating a new estimate", names unit, quantity, selling price, hour estimate, markup, discount, assignee and hourly cost rate, with projected cost/profit/margin totals. "Accessing the estimate builder" restricts default access to Owners and Partners. "Create a project from an Estimate" can initialize budget/dates/team and optionally create tasks or resource allocations. This broader scope is evidence of required inputs, not evidence that Quits customers want all those modules.

Contrary evidence is preserved. Bonsai already separates budget actual use from remaining estimates and allows budget inclusion settings. Its estimate builder has an explicit cost rate, so it is inaccurate to claim all its projections confuse selling price with cost. The narrower risk is exclusion of unfinished overrun/unestimated work under the documented remaining-task conditions. Feature presence gives no adoption, accuracy or willingness-to-pay evidence for Quits.

## Proposed definitions, units and grain

Every result carries organization, reporting group or agreement, source IDs/revisions, currency/unit, as-of timestamp and selected inclusion policy. Use organization time zone to define a half-open period `[start, end)`. Separate cumulative project-to-date measures from period flows. For effort, store duration in minutes or integer seconds and convert to decimal hours for display; never add days and hours without an explicit day-length convention. Synthetic fixtures use exact whole hours. Monetary fixtures use integer minor DKK units at exponent 2; documentation examples use DKK major units.

| Measure | Grain and formula | Inclusion and exclusions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accepted contract value | One accepted offer revision per agreement; sum frozen net service prices in its currency. | Exclude drafts, declined offers, duplicate revisions and schedule/deposit cash-request lines from service value. Include actual accepted service scope, with cancellation policy explicit. Do not label it earned income or profit. |
| Eligible-to-bill work | Distinct approved source/revision and allocation generation as of the report; frozen net price of eligible unreserved work. | Apply reviewed trigger and dispute/approval policy. Exclude cancelled, rejected, disputed, reserved and already invoiced work, including fixed-fee-included time entries to avoid double billing. #49 must define this before release. |
| Net issued service value | Issued sale invoice net amounts minus issued sale-credit net amounts. Flow by issue date; cumulative form separately named. | Exclude drafts, void/nonissued documents and prepayments from service subtotal. Keep VAT outside the net amount. Credits reduce issued value, not cash automatically. |
| Customer funding and bank cash | Unique active receipt; gross funding, processor fee and net received shown separately by receipt payment date. Refunds reduce the corresponding cash flow, reversals retain provenance. | Exclude drafts, anticipated payments and confirmed processing from received cash. Never sum an allocation again as another receipt. Keep legacy invoice payments separately labeled until receipt evidence exists. |
| Debt discharge and outstanding | Active allocation per invoice; outstanding `max(gross - issued gross credits - active paid amount, 0)`. | Gross and invoice currency. Exclude reversed allocations. A project subtotal attributes discharges, not independent funding. Unapplied customer funds remain separate. |
| Recorded effort | Unique source entry/revision, person, work date, unit and work identity; sum valid approved durations as of the cutoff. | Include billable and nonbillable project labor for a stated cost scope. Exclude duplicates, deleted/rejected entries and overlapping entries needing review. Label late or incomplete logs. This is recorded actual effort, not proof of all work performed. |
| Remaining effort | One owner-supplied low/high estimate-to-complete per unfinished work item and as-of revision. | Incremental future hours, never lifetime estimate. Include known rework. Missing, stale, unreliable or unmapped work yields unknown. Complete effort has zero remaining; cancellation requires owner confirmation of any closeout work. |
| Projected billable value | Remaining eligible hourly work multiplied by its explicit selling rate and authorization/cap policy; optionally actual-plus-remaining pricing scenario. | Exclude time included in fixed fees/retainers, VAT and unauthorised overrun. Not an invoice, receivable, cash forecast or profit. Do not add it to the same fixed-fee contract value. |
| Recorded direct cost | Approved source hours multiplied by dated internal cost rates, plus evidenced direct expenses under the declared recoverable-tax policy. | Cost-rate coverage must be complete for an all-cost figure. Exclude unsupported overhead, FX and unknown expense-tax treatment; name the subtotal. Fixed customer prices are not cost rates. |
| Forecast direct cost and contribution | Recorded scoped costs plus remaining-hour scenario times dated internal rates and explicit remaining expense scenarios. Contribution is accepted scoped net fee minus forecast scoped direct cost. | Unknown required inputs block total cost/contribution. Exclude overhead, tax, FX, financing and unpriced scope unless explicitly collected. Call the result direct contribution, not accounting profit. Percentage divides by the stated fee, never by cash received; zero fee yields no percentage. |

Mixed currencies remain separate. A dated, explicit valuation rule is required before combining them; invoice historical FX is not a bank-rate assumption. Missing hours/rates are null, never zero. Work, invoices and cash overlap in lineage and cannot be added into a grand total. Cost/rate read permissions must be independently enforced in backend queries and exports, not just hidden in the UI.

## Remaining-effort rule and numerical replay

An original estimate records what someone expected at planning time. Recorded actuals say what the source has logged. Neither establishes how much remains today. An unfinished item without a fresh owner estimate-to-complete is unknown even when actual hours are still below its original estimate. `max(original - actual, 0)` may be shown only as a clearly labeled original-plan residual, never as an assured remaining-effort value.

| Work state | Original estimate, hours | Recorded actual, hours | Owner remaining low/high, hours | Total actual-plus-remaining, hours |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | --- |
| Unfinished overrun | 10 | 12 | Unknown | Blocked |
| Same overrun after owner review | 10 | 12 | 2 to 4 | 14 to 16 |
| Completed overrun | 10 | 12 | 0 | 12 |
| Incomplete below estimate | 10 | 4 | Unknown | Blocked |
| Unestimated unfinished work | Unknown | 3 | Unknown | Blocked |
| Unfinished, owner explicitly confirms no further effort | 10 | 12 | 0 to 0, with rationale | 12; completion/acceptance state still separate |

The 2-to-4-hour range is a scenario supplied by an owner, not a confidence interval or predicted distribution. The original 10-hour estimate remains retained. Relative to it, the reviewed total overrun is 4 to 6 hours. A completed item can have 12 actual hours against 10 estimated without any forecast uncertainty. Reopening it requires a new remaining-effort review.

For a synthetic project containing unfinished overrun 12 hours, completed work 12 hours and unestimated work 3 hours, known recorded effort is 27 hours. Two remaining estimates are unknown, so a complete projected total or margin is blocked. Show the known actual subtotal and unknown item count with drill-down. Excluding unknown tasks would produce false precision. A known partial scenario sum must be labeled incomplete and has no complete high bound. Do not claim budget safety from it.

A separate DKK example uses a fixed net fee of 12,000, issued sale net 8,000 and a net credit of 1,000. Net issued service value is 7,000; with an explicit 25% VAT scenario the gross debt basis is 8,750. Gross receipt funding is 4,000, fee 100 and net bank cash 3,900. Allocating 4,000 leaves 4,750 gross due. None of those values is a profit measure.

For the reviewed 12-actual plus 2-to-4 remaining-hour scenario, internal rate 300 DKK/hour and evidenced direct expense 500 DKK produce recorded direct cost 4,100 DKK and remaining labor cost 600 to 1,200 DKK. Forecast scoped direct cost is 4,700 to 5,300 DKK. Against the fixed fee, scoped contribution is 6,700 to 7,300 DKK, or approximately 55.83% to 60.83%. This excludes processor fees, overhead, taxes, FX and scope changes and is not accounting profit. The separately illustrated hourly selling scenario at 1,000 DKK/hour is 14,000 to 16,000 DKK. It does not change the agreed fixed fee and must not be added to it.

## Input quality and blocked forecasts

A later source adapter needs stable organization/provider/external-entry identity, explicit revisions and deletion handling, timezone-aware work dates, duration units, person/work mapping, billable/included classification and approval. Imported changes must request review and must never rewrite frozen invoice lines. #73's allocation contract can govern billable consumption; cost analysis must also include nonbillable work without consuming it for billing.

A valid forecast needs a bounded, reconciled work inventory as well as logs. Mark it blocked when any in-scope work lacks trustworthy actuals, completion status, current remaining estimate, cost rate or required expense estimate. An item due outside a budget cycle remains visible as out-of-cycle work, not silently complete. Unknown dates block date-based forecasts but need not erase valid hours. A missing cost rate blocks cost/contribution, while valid recorded hours can still be displayed. Missing selling rate blocks billable-value scenarios, not recorded effort. Log coverage gaps must distinguish unavailable source from a positively evidenced zero.

For the pilot, treat remaining estimates older than seven days or predating a material scope/status/source change as needing review. That seven-day threshold is a proposed study setting, not a researched industry rule. Obtain dated internal rates and define which labor/expense categories they include. Do not collect payroll records. Until a qualified reviewer approves expense tax and FX semantics, use explicitly scoped same-currency direct-cost subtotals or block the total. The receipts draft already documents that those tax/valuation gaps are unresolved.

## Six-business study, not yet conducted

Recruit six consenting Danish service businesses, with at least three billing primarily by time and three managing fixed-fee work with time estimates. Record size, business model and current tools without treating this sample as representative of Denmark. The parent owns recruitment and any external communication. No contacts or interviews happened during this task.

For each business, observe one recent estimation or billing failure in its real workflow, with permission. Ask the owner to show the original estimate, later logs, unfinished/rework status, warning timing, billing outcome and current workaround. Record what they knew at each point rather than reconstructing hindsight estimates. Ask them to perform the proposed separation of effort, remaining scenario, billed value and cash using their own example. Record errors, time taken, data they cannot supply and whether they will actually use an existing-source import plus a weekly remaining review. A stated interest is weaker evidence than agreeing to and completing the pilot.

Suggested interview prompts are: What changed after the original estimate? When did you first know the work would overrun? What entry or spreadsheet made it visible? Which hours were included in a fixed fee or became nonbillable rework? Who can supply remaining effort, and how often? Who may see rates and margins? Which tool is authoritative for actuals? What would prevent weekly use, and what concrete next project would you use this on? Record contrary examples where the current workflow catches overruns adequately or additional review takes too much effort.

The evidence record per business needs consent scope, workflow observed, date, anonymized failure, current workaround, source availability, adoption commitment/refusal and observation versus interpretation. Do not invent quotes. Keep identifiable customer books and cost rates outside public OSS; publish only permitted anonymized conclusions. Consent and evidence retention policy must be agreed before collecting artifacts. Customer research acceptance remains unmet until these records exist.

## Historical replay and measurable pilot design

With consent, replay at least two recent projects per business, at least 12 total. Use only entries and estimates available at each historical cutoff. Preserve source revision and update time. If historical remaining estimates were never recorded, label them unavailable; an owner's retrospective recollection cannot validate a past forecast. Record all missing or unreliable inputs and excluded projects, with denominator counts. Compare the first valid warning time and actionable decision against the prior workflow, not just against a final project total.

Then run a four-week prospective pilot with one agreed source and at least one active project per participating business. Do not select a provider or implement an adapter merely because it appears in competitor docs. Pre-register the following proposed thresholds with the parent before recruitment; they are decision gates, not measured results:

| Question | Metric and proposed gate |
| --- | --- |
| Is there repeated demand? | At least 4 of 6 businesses show a consented recent failure that the existing workflow missed or caught late, and at least 4 complete the four-week pilot rather than only stating interest. Retain refusals and adequate-workflow counterexamples. |
| Can inputs be trusted? | All imported pilot entries retain stable identity and reconcile counts and durations to the source at each cutoff; no duplicates. At least 95% of in-scope active work has current actual coverage and reviewed remaining estimates. The other work must visibly block complete forecasts. Cost projections require 100% rate/expense-policy coverage for their declared scope. |
| Is the warning useful? | Compare warning timestamps and decisions for all eligible replay/pilot projects. Seek at least one independently documented useful earlier warning in each of 3 businesses; report cases where warnings are later, false, ignored or impossible. No synthetic range counts toward this gate. |
| Is the review affordable? | Median owner weekly review time at most 10 minutes per active project, measured across actual pilot sessions, and 4 of 6 businesses voluntarily continue the workflow at the end. Record setup/mapping time separately. |
| Does uncertainty remain visible? | Every unfinished 10/12-hour or unestimated case with no reviewed remaining estimate shows unknown; no incomplete project is labeled within budget. Check every such case, not a sample. |
| Does it preserve billing and privacy? | Zero duplicated billed sources, issued-document changes or unauthorized rate exposure. Reconcile imported eligible/excluded work, issued lines, receipt funding and allocations separately. Any violation blocks release. |

Report retrospective sample counts, missing-input rate, known forecast errors and warning false positives with their actual denominator. For prospectively reviewed ranges, record whether final remaining effort fell inside them and the absolute difference from each bound. These are descriptive results on a small selected sample, not a general accuracy promise. If fewer than 12 usable historical projects exist, record that shortfall; do not replace them with synthetic forecasts. Unreliable source coverage can stop the forecast pilot while interviews and #49 reconciliation proceed.

## Go/no-go and bounded implementation if warranted

Current decision is defer. No customer-demand, historical-warning or accuracy gate is met. Public primary-source research and synthetic arithmetic are complete; accountant/customer/provider evidence remains explicitly unmet.

Choose integrate if the study gates pass, one repeatedly used time source has a supported read contract with stable revisions and consented access, #49 has shipped with reconciled measures, and privacy/cost policy is reviewed. Billing consumption reuses #73 identity and reservation rules; cash semantics reuse #72. It must refuse incomplete all-cost totals and preserve issued artifacts. It includes no native timer or resource planning. Backend/contracts/tests come first, with UX-owned screens later.

Choose no-go if owners cannot supply remaining reviews, the source cannot reconcile reliably, demand is already met by their existing workflow, warnings do not change decisions or weekly burden exceeds the gate. Keep the narrower #49 work-money view. A native timer would require separate evidence of a repeated source gap that integration cannot solve and a new scope decision; nothing here authorizes it. Change this decision only on recorded customer evidence and completed data prerequisites, never on competitor feature counts.
