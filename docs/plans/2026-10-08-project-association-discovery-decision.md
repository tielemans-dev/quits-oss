# Project association discovery decision

Issue [#55](https://github.com/tielemans-dev/quits-oss/issues/55), 8 October 2026. Status: proposed reporting policy for parent review. No project runtime, schema, contracts or UI change accompanies this decision.

## Decision

Keep a reporting association separate from the legal buyer, issued document and billing provenance. Assignment, removal and correction must change only internal reporting metadata. They must preserve invoice identity, artifacts, payments, receipt funding, debt, reminder inputs and agreement-work allocations. Refuse association to another customer or organization. A request to change a buyer, contractual scope, price, VAT or issued wording belongs to a separately reviewed financial-document correction workflow. This document does not establish that workflow or offer legal approval.

Existing agreement links already support grouping invoices from one agreement. First test whether reporting by agreement solves the need. Only historical manual invoices or grouping several agreements for the same customer justify a separate project label. If validated, the smallest addition is a customer-bound reporting group and an independently revisioned invoice association. It has no tasks, resource planning, timer, payroll or cost ledger.

## Why separate association matters

Intuit's ["Add existing invoices to projects"](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/learn-support/en-us/help-article/invoicing/add-existing-invoices-quickbooks-online-projects/L5awONEiL_US_en_US), sections "Learn more about paid invoices before editing" and "Step 3: Reconnect the payment to the invoice", says adding a paid invoice disconnects its payment and makes it unpaid. It instructs operators to reconnect the payment and turn off automatic credit application first. This is a documented QuickBooks workflow, not an observed Quits defect.

Intuit's ["Create projects"](https://quickbooks.intuit.com/learn-support/en-us/help-article/manage-projects/set-create-projects-quickbooks-online/L9GAdLMyT_US_en_US), introduction and "Create a new project", groups transactions for a single customer. Contrary evidence matters: its project form allows billing details specific to a project, and its video describes changing the customer. Quits should not inherit those changes through an internal label. No comparison of product quality or Danish legal suitability follows from these docs.

Both official pages were read on 2026-10-08 through the `r.jina.ai` HTML reader. Direct Intuit requests timed out. Citations name the first-party URLs and exact sections; reader transport is not an independent factual authority.

## Proposed assignment policy

The first version permits reporting association only after invoice issuance. A draft has no association: assignment, correction and removal requests against a draft refuse without mutation. Commit must recheck issuance and the frozen buyer under the same transaction/lock as association validation. Issuing a draft does not auto-assign a project; a later explicit reporting command reviews the issued document. This keeps the existing draft contact-change path outside the association invariant. Supporting draft associations later requires a separate design that validates or clears the association atomically on every buyer change, with permission and concurrency tests; it is not authorized by this proposal.

One issued invoice has at most one explicit reporting project. The group is organization-scoped and has an immutable customer binding once used. The invoice's contact remains its billing link; its frozen buyer remains issued evidence. A same-contact match is necessary, but mismatched or missing historical buyer evidence requires review rather than reconstruction from today's contact. Duplicate contacts or a later buyer-identity change must not bypass this check. A group can contain several agreements for that customer, but invoice membership never rewrites their relationships. Do not infer membership dynamically from an editable agreement label after an explicit invoice assignment.

Assignment starts from unassigned, removal returns to unassigned and correction replaces one same-customer group with another. Each action records previous/next group, reason, actor, timestamp, command identity and association revision. Archived groups retain historical evidence; reject new assignments to them and permit removal. All three actions require corroborated frozen buyer evidence, including removal; missing or conflicting evidence defers the action for review without rewriting the invoice or reconstructing its buyer. A preview must name the actual invoices and totals, and commit must require the reviewed association revision. Organization-scoped lookup refuses a foreign group as not found. Cross-customer assignment refuses atomically. Bulk changes, if later needed, require sorted locks and all-or-nothing validation, rather than a partially reassigned batch.

Only the association row and its audit event may change. Preserve issued number and dates, contact, buyer/seller snapshots, issuance snapshot, currency, valuation, amounts, VAT/price basis, payment instructions, PDF/UBL refs, bytes and hashes. Preserve every payment, receipt, refund, allocation, checkout identity, credit, reminder history, pause/policy input, source revision and allocation generation. Do not send email, touch public tokens or change customer-facing descriptions. Missing artifact hashes are a migration/evidence gap; assignment must not fabricate or regenerate them.

A reporting move changes attribution, not money. The organization's totals stay equal; old and new project buckets change by equal opposite amounts. An explicit unassigned bucket prevents disappearing history. Reports group each currency separately and state the membership basis using one organization-wide committed association-event sequence cutoff. An invoice's association revision is only a concurrency token for that invoice; it cannot be a report-wide historical cutoff. Document issue date and receipt date remain financial dates; assignment date never substitutes for either.

The proposed association event stores both the per-invoice revision and an organization-wide sequence. A future writer must serialize sequence allocation and the association change within the same organization-locked transaction, so sequence order agrees with visible committed history. A report captures the highest visible committed sequence in its database snapshot, or selects a previously recorded cutoff, and applies that one cutoff to every invoice. For each invoice, use its last committed association event at or below the cutoff; an explicit removal event means unassigned, as does having no event yet. Batch changes must appear atomically. The report records organization, sequence cutoff, financial period and inclusion policy. A timestamp may help the reader, but timestamp ties or clock skew cannot choose membership. Existing arbitrary audit IDs are not assumed to supply this ordering. This is a future contract, not an implemented sequence or a claim about the synthetic helper.

This sequence cutoff freezes membership, not financial amounts; reproducible historical financial totals also need a separately reviewed recorded-at cutoff or revision inclusion policy for late credits, backdated receipts and reversals before implementation.

For example, events A revision 1 at sequence 10, B revision 1 at 11, A revision 2 at 12 and B revision 2 at 13 give A revision 2 plus B revision 1 at cutoff 12. A shared per-invoice revision number cannot express that state. A current report captures the latest committed cutoff once, so a concurrent move cannot mix membership from before and after the report's snapshot. The existing synthetic assignment replay does not prove this multi-invoice history or transaction isolation; runtime integration must test both before reporting ships.

Credits follow their original invoice's selected reporting association, with original credit identity intact. Cash allocation attribution follows the invoice allocation. A receipt split across projects is counted once as funding in the receipt ledger. A project report can show attributed debt discharge and receipt lineage, but must not describe summed allocations as additional bank cash. Leave unapplied customer funds in a separate customer/unassigned bucket. Do not distribute receipt fees or residuals across projects without a reviewed allocation rule.

Existing financial CSVs and event payloads retain their values and ordering. A future separate reporting-association export should carry organization, invoice ID, project ID or explicit removal, per-invoice revision, organization-wide sequence, reason, recorded audit timestamp and event ID. A report export also names its shared sequence cutoff. Downstream consumers can update a reporting dimension by that identity and replay order; they must not reverse/repost a financial entry, relink payment or alter exported issued evidence. A downstream provider that conflates project and customer needs its own tested adapter policy. No QuickBooks or e-conomic write was attempted.

## Minimal follow-ups and Kvit handoff

1. Validate the need for grouping manual history or several agreements; offer existing agreement reporting first. Obtain consenting customer examples and qualified accounting review of downstream dimension changes. Neither has happened.
2. If needed, design only the internal group, association revision and audit contract. Implement one organization-scoped command for issued invoices, independent of invoice editing/issuance. Recheck issued state and frozen buyer at commit; drafts remain unassigned. Reuse command permissions, transaction/idempotency conventions and reviewed previews. No implicit financial permission elevation.
3. Integrate merged #73, #72 and corrective #80, then test real artifact byte/hash identity, payment/receipt conservation, group-bucket reconciliation, concurrent stale previews, customer/tenant refusal and unchanged reminder eligibility. Include assignment before/after a draft buyer change, stale draft-to-issued previews, and legacy missing-evidence refusal cases.
4. Add the separate read/export reporting dimension only after consumer semantics are reviewed. A full project-management model is unnecessary.

UX owns the Kvit identity and all screens. Its eventual internal affordance should label "Reporting project", show the unchanged legal buyer and invoice number, offer same-customer groups and unassigned, and review assignment/removal/correction with a reason. Offer the action only on issued invoices; drafts explain that reporting assignment becomes available after issuance. Show report currency, membership basis and unassigned totals. Preserve draft numberlessness. A refusal should explain why a legal-buyer change needs a separate workflow. The action must not enter the invoice editor, alter document-view contracts or show internal reporting metadata on customer documents. No layout, branding or public-page change is proposed here.
